# Voice profile

A stored profile means the rewrite matches the writer from the first draft instead of the third. Offer this once to a writer who works with the skill regularly and has no sample on record. Do not push it twice, and do not build one from a single passage.

Fill it from real writing the person has already published or sent. Everything here is observed, not aspirational: what they do, not what they think good writing is.

---

## Samples

Paste two or three passages of 150 words or more, from different contexts if possible (one informal, one work). These outrank every rule in the skill, including the dash rule.

```
Sample 1 (context: ...)


Sample 2 (context: ...)
```

## Observed

**Sentence length.** Typical range, and whether short and long alternate or stay even.

**Paragraph length.** Lines per paragraph. Whether one-sentence paragraphs appear, and whether they carry content or emphasis.

**Openings.** How sentences and paragraphs start. Subject-first, action-first, conjunctions at the start of a sentence.

**Contractions.** Always, never, or mixed by register.

**Punctuation.** Dashes: used, and at what rate. Semicolons, colons, parentheses, ellipses. Straight or curly quotes. Oxford comma.

**Spelling convention.** British, American, or mixed by audience.

**Person and stance.** First person singular or plural. Whether opinions appear as opinions ("my read is") or as statements.

**Hedging.** How uncertainty is expressed, and how often.

**Humour and asides.** Present or absent. Dry, self-deprecating, none.

**Formatting habits.** Bullets versus prose. Bold. Headings. Emoji.

**Vocabulary.** Words they reach for. Words they clearly avoid.

**Quirks to preserve.** Anything that would look like an error to a copy editor and is actually them.

## Do not imitate

Things in the samples that the writer would not want carried forward: a typo, a phrase from a document template, jargon that belonged to one employer, a tic they have said they are trying to drop.

## Channel notes

Where their register genuinely differs by destination, note it here rather than assuming the samples generalise. For example: informal in Slack, plain and unhedged in specs, first person and opinionated in long-form.

---

**How to use it.** Read the samples first and the observations second. The samples are evidence; the observations are a summary of them and can be wrong. Where the two disagree, follow the samples.
