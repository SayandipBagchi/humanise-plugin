# Meeting pack

The detail behind `SKILL.md`. Read it for anything beyond a single line. It does not repeat the output structure or the speakable-lines rules; those live in `SKILL.md`.

## Pin down the four things

Before writing a line, know these. Ask about the ones you cannot infer, up to three questions, and state an assumption for the rest rather than stalling.

- **Audience.** Who is listening, and what do they carry responsibility for?
- **Decision.** What should they decide, approve, understand, or do by the end?
- **Evidence.** What facts, examples, mechanisms, or sources are actually available?
- **Constraint.** How much time, budget, risk, authority, or attention is in the room?

The most common miss is *decision*. "I need to update them on the project" is not a decision; "I need them to approve two weeks of discovery" is.

## Order the argument

The Pyramid Principle is top-down: one answer at the top, the strongest supporting reasons grouped underneath, evidence and detail under each reason.

- Keep reasons distinct. Two reasons that restate each other read as one weak reason.
- The strongest reason normally comes first, unless the audience needs a different order to follow it.
- Use a clean grouping where it helps. Do not force unrelated ideas into a neat three-part list to make the structure look finished.

BLUF, bottom line up front, is the spoken version of the same discipline: the first sentence tells the listener the point or the decision needed. Context follows only when it earns its place.

## Build each point

Use this chain:

**Claim -> proof -> implication -> ask**

- **Claim.** What are you saying?
- **Proof.** What in the supplied material supports it?
- **Implication.** Why does it matter to this audience specifically?
- **Ask.** What should happen next?

Keep the categories separate in the output. Blurring them is how a recommendation ends up sounding like evidence.

### Language for thin evidence

These read as confident, not weak, because they are precise about what is known:

- "What we know is..."
- "The current data shows..."
- "My read is..."
- "The risk is..."
- "We have not measured that yet."
- "I would test this before claiming the outcome."

## Match the stakeholder

### Senior leader or decision-maker

Lead with the decision, the business effect, the risk, the options, and the ask. Keep implementation detail ready for questions rather than leading with it.

- "My recommendation is ___ because ___ and ___."
- "The decision I need today is ___."
- "The main risk is ___; the way to contain it is ___."
- "We can do ___ now, but ___ remains unmeasured."

### Product, engineering, or operations partner

Lead with the problem, the mechanism, the constraint, the trade-off, and the owner. Name what changes in the system or the workflow.

- "The problem is ___ at ___."
- "The change would work by ___."
- "The trade-off is ___; I am choosing it because ___."
- "The part I need from your team is ___."

### Cross-functional stakeholder

Start with the shared outcome, then the dependency and the decision. Make ownership visible.

- "We are trying to achieve ___."
- "The dependency is ___, and it sits with ___."
- "If we do not decide ___, the next step waits."
- "I am asking for ___ by ___."

### Customer, partner, or external audience

Lead with their problem and the observable outcome. Avoid internal jargon, unsupported promises, and explanations of your process unless asked.

- "The problem this addresses is ___."
- "What changes for you is ___."
- "The current limitation is ___."
- "The next step is ___; we will know it worked when ___."

## The stakeholder update variant

A status update is not a pitch, and the default talk-track shape in `SKILL.md` is a pitch. For an update, return:

- **Bottom line.** What changed, and why this listener should care.
- **What we know.** Concrete facts.
- **What it means.** The bounded interpretation, marked as interpretation.
- **Risk or gap.** What is still unknown.
- **Need from you.** The decision or action, or explicitly "nothing, this is for awareness".

The last line matters. An update with no stated ask makes the room invent one.

## Handle pushback

1. Acknowledge the actual concern, not a softer version of it.
2. Answer it directly.
3. Give the evidence or the trade-off.
4. State the boundary, meaning what you are not claiming.
5. Offer the next step.

Shape:

- "That concern is fair."
- "The reason I still recommend this is ___."
- "The evidence we have is ___."
- "I am not claiming ___ yet."
- "We can resolve the open question by ___."

Do not invent a stakeholder's motive, and do not pre-empt an objection nobody has raised. If the answer is unknown, say so and propose how to find it.

Common shapes worth preparing for: "we cannot fit this in", "do we have data on that", "why now", "what does this cost us", "who owns it", "what happens if we do nothing". The last one is usually the strongest card and the least prepared.

## Time-box the answer

Fit the structure to the time available:

- **30 seconds:** answer, two reasons, ask.
- **2 minutes:** answer, three reasons with evidence, trade-off, ask.
- **5 minutes:** answer, short SCQ if needed, grouped reasons, evidence, options, risk, ask, then likely questions.

Do not supply longer versions unless they are useful or requested. A user who asked for thirty seconds and received five minutes has to do the cutting themselves, in the corridor.
